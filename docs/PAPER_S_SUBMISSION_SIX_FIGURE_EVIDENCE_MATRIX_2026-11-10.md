## Paper scope and central falsifiable statement

*Working scope:* Identifiability and robustness of synthetic-resource battery storage dispatch on source-ordinal projected Western U.S. electricity demand. With the original EFS/LAF demand and tightly controlled illustrative LP, **system-wide modeled annual aggregate objective and annual delivered battery energy may be reproducible under fixed assumptions while the within-year allocation of dispatch can be poorly identified by the primary LP objective**. The independent MW/MWh design also identifies regions of model-defined capacity complementarity and saturation. These findings are **model-conditional** and do not estimate real renewable curtailment, storage economics, reliability performance, or actual network-constrained Western OPF.

## Proposed compact manuscript sections and evidence alignment

1. **Introduction** — tension between aggregate planning sensitivity and temporal dispatch identifiability, with a focused related-work argument rather than broad empirical energy-transition claims.
2. **Data/provenance** — EFS/LAF original source, seven Western states, 20 zones, **seven distinct normalized nonzero shape groups**. Explain source ordinal, 2040 label issue, source permission and synthetic supply.
3. **Methods** — exact original objective `5 × (direct renewable MWh + battery renewable charge-input MWh) + 90 × firm MWh`; all hourly load/SOC constraints and 90% roundtrip; zero initial/terminal SOC; 540-case 0/4/8h baseline; 189-case independent P/E and 84 interactions; cost sublevel set `C(x) <= C* + max(USD 0.05, 10^-9 × C*)`; distinguish model shadow prices from investment values.
4. **Results A** — Fig. 2–4 resource-timing and duration evidence; aggregation and nonindependence qualifier.
5. **Results B** — Fig. 5 independent power–energy factor design, interactions, saturation and counterexample.
6. **Results C** — Fig. 6 aggregate versus window discharge identification, with source-hour 4001–4168 and full-year displacement residuals.
7. **Discussion** — what is identifiable from the model and what is not; battery economics, real resource availability, actual interzonal electrical network and UTC remain out of scope; explain why canonical minimum-annual-discharge convention is not unique physical operations.
8. **Conclusions** — transparent source-bounded computational method, **not** a real-grid predictive dispatch recommendation.

## Reviewer red-team issues to address before manuscript freeze

1. **Novelty:** source-driven hypothetical factor sweeps are not sufficient on their own; emphasize verified *identifiability split* and cross-constraint checks as the substantive bounded insight; compare against LP dispatch degeneracy and storage-model benchmark literature.
2. **Pseudoreplication:** 20 zones = 20 zonal load series, **not 20 independent demand chronologies**; seven nonzero normalized classes only. Across-year profiles share source construction.
3. **Interpretability:** toy annual objective is operational dispatch cost only, excludes batteries' investment, degradation and transmission; never call marginal objective reduction real savings.
4. **Time:** no certified original EFS UTC clock; do not label source 168-hour windows as physical weeks or source 24h blocks as calendar days.
5. **Identification:** cost-capped extrema are near-optimal **within nonzero original-cost allowance and finite-precision HiGHS**, not certified exact multiple-optimum solutions.
6. **External validity:** assumed ordinal Wave A/B and flat profiles are not actual PV/wind/observed curtailment, so avoid real Western Interconnection policy claims.
7. **Redundancy:** 20 working S-figure IDs should not become a 20-figure main text; each main figure must answer a distinct stated research question.
8. **Manuscript privacy:** keep full text and coauthor review drafts private; only public methods/derived facts and accepted figure instructions in this repository.
